Application Requirements – Breathe & Play Madrid

Hands-on 1 · Open Data and Knowledge Graphs · An application built on a knowledge graph of air quality and sports facilities in Madrid

1. Application overview

Breathe & Play Madrid is a web application that helps people choose where and when to do outdoor sport in Madrid, taking air quality into account. It is built on a knowledge graph that links:

  • the 607 basic municipal sports facilities, most of them open-air (D3);
  • the 24 air quality monitoring stations (D2);
  • their daily pollutant measurements for 2024 (D1);
  • external entities from Wikidata: districts, pollutants, sports, metro stations and landmark places.

Each facility is linked to its nearest monitoring station, so the app can show the air quality around it. Users can search facilities by sport or district, see how often the air near a facility exceeds the limit values, and compare districts by sports supply and air quality.

2. Target users

UserNeed
Amateur athlete / runnerFind a nearby facility for their sport where the air is usually cleanest.
Sensitive person (asthma, elderly people, families with children)Know how many days the air near a facility exceeds the limit values, to plan outdoor activities.
Sports club / schoolChoose facilities for training sessions, taking into account accessibility, public transport and air quality.
City planner / journalistCompare districts by number of sports facilities and air quality, and identify facilities in the most polluted areas.

3. Functional requirements

IDRequirementData used
FR1Display the sports facilities and the air quality monitoring stations on a map.D2, D3
FR2Filter facilities by sport or facility type (football, futsal, calisthenics, skating, pétanque…), district, neighbourhood and accessibility.D3
FR3Color each facility according to the air quality measured at its nearest station for the selected pollutant and date.D1, D2, D3
FR4Show a facility's detail page: address, district, neighbourhood, equipment, accessibility, public transport, link to the official web page and nearest station (with its distance).D2, D3
FR5On the facility page, show the daily evolution of the pollutants measured by the nearest station and the number of days in the year that exceeded the limit value (e.g. daily PM10 mean > 50 µg/m³).D1, D2, D3
FR6Recommend the facilities of a given sport with the best air quality, either in a district or near a point chosen by the user.D1, D2, D3
FR7Rank districts by number of sports facilities and by the average air quality of the stations assigned to them.D1, D2, D3
FR8Show linked information from Wikidata for districts, pollutants, sports and metro stations (description and link).Wikidata
FR9Exclude values flagged as not valid (N) from all calculations and indicate this in the interface.D1
FR10Show the source and license of the data (CC BY 4.0, Madrid City Council).Metadata

4. Non-functional requirements

IDRequirement
NFR1All data is queried through SPARQL over the knowledge graph generated from the CSV files.
NFR2The application runs in a web browser and is responsive on mobile devices.
NFR3Each query issued by the interface returns in under 3 seconds with one year of data.
NFR4Standard vocabularies are reused where possible: SOSA/SSN for observations, GeoSPARQL/WGS84 for locations, schema.org for sports facilities and xsd:date for dates.
NFR5The attribution required by the CC BY 4.0 license is displayed.
NFR6The interface is available in English and Spanish and uses accessible colors: pollution levels are never conveyed by color alone.

5. Competency questions

  1. Which calisthenics facilities are in the district of Retiro, and which monitoring station is closest to each?
  2. What was the daily NO₂ value at the station nearest to a given facility on 15/01/2024?
  3. On how many days in 2024 did the station nearest to each football pitch exceed a daily PM10 mean of 50 µg/m³?
  4. Which district has the most sports facilities, and what is the average NO₂ of its stations?
  5. Which accessible facilities are near an “urban background” station rather than an “urban traffic” one?
  6. What is the pollutant with code 14, and what is its Wikidata entity?

7. Out of scope

  • Real-time air quality data and forecasts.
  • Booking facilities or checking their opening hours: the dataset barely contains opening hours.
  • Measuring air quality at the facility itself: the app always approximates it using the nearest station.
  • User accounts and personalised notifications.
