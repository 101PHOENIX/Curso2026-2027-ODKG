Dataset Requirements

Hands-on 1 · Open Data and Knowledge Graphs · Group 08 · Domain: urban environment and climate adaptation in the city of Barcelona

The project builds a knowledge graph of the public resources that Barcelona offers to cope with heat: climate shelters, public drinking fountains and street trees. This document analyses whether the selected datasets satisfy the hands-on requirements (R1-R4 mandatory, R5-R6 optional). The datasets were checked on 1 October 2026.

3. Analysis of each dataset

D1 - Climate shelters network

  • R1. Urban environment and climate adaptation: public spaces and facilities where citizens can protect themselves from heat.
  • R2. CSV file with 550 records and 32 columns, comma separated and encoded in UTF-16 (converted to UTF-8 in csv/). Main columns: register_id (unique identifier), name, institution_id and institution_name (host institution, present in 110 records), created, modified, address columns (addresses_road_id, addresses_road_name, street numbers, addresses_neighborhood_id and _name, addresses_district_id and _name, zip code, town), contact attributes (values_*), coordinates (geo_epgs_25831_x/y in ETRS89 and geo_epgs_4326_lat/lon in WGS84) and timetable (opening hours as an HTML table).
  • R3. CC BY 4.0, shown on the dataset page.
  • R4. All 550 records have neighbourhood (73 different), district (10 different), street and coordinates, so they can be linked to administrative areas. Some records are well-known public facilities (libraries, municipal markets, parks, museums, swimming pools) that can have Wikidata items.
  • R5. Description in the portal metadata; no field-level documentation was found.
  • Content. The dataset mixes several kinds of places and has no column with the type of place. Judging by the names, it includes parks and gardens, swimming pools, libraries, senior citizens' centres, civic centres, sports centres, markets, supermarkets, pharmacies, museums, shops, hospitals and clinics, schools and associations. The category will be derived from the name in the data preparation. About 35 records are named "Refugi climatic" and belong to a host institution, and about 94 records share street and number with another record, so a facility can appear in more than one record.

D2 - Drinking fountains

  • R1. Urban environment and public infrastructure: water points available in streets and parks.
  • R2. CSV file with 1,760 records and 12 columns, UTF-8: CODI (unique identifier), NOM, CARRER, NUMERO_CARRER, CODI_BARRI, NOM_BARRI, CODI_DISTRICTE, NOM_DISTRICTE, X_ETRS89, Y_ETRS89, LATITUD and LONGITUD. The portal publishes one version per year, released each March.
  • R3. CC BY 4.0, shown on the dataset page.
  • R4. All records have coordinates and district; 1,757 have a street and 1,750 have a neighbourhood (73 neighbourhoods, 10 districts).
  • R5. The catalogue lists the fields of the file; no further documentation was found.
  • Content. 349 records have no name, 41 have no street number and 3 have no street.

D3 - Street trees

  • R1. Urban environment: street trees provide shade and reduce temperature, noise and CO2.
  • R2. CSV file of about 42 MB with 22 columns, included in full in csv/. The portal warns that it is large and that some browsers fail to download it. Historical files with the data of the last week of each quarter are also published.
  • R3. CC BY 4.0, shown on the dataset page.
  • R4. Columns: codi (unique identifier), x_etrs89, y_etrs89, latitud, longitud, tipus_element (street tree or street palm), espai_verd, adreca, cat_especie_id, cat_nom_cientific, cat_nom_castella, cat_nom_catala, categoria_arbrat, data_plantacio, tipus_aigua, tipus_reg, geom, catalogacio, codi_barri, nom_barri, codi_districte and nom_districte. Neighbourhood and district allow linking to administrative areas; the scientific name allows linking to taxa.
  • R5. The portal gives a field-by-field description of the current and the previous structure of the file and explains the 2021 changes. The species columns have the prefix cat_ in the file, which the description does not show.

5. Licence analysis

  • Rightsholder and publisher. Ajuntament de Barcelona, through Open Data BCN. The datasets are also listed in the Spanish national catalogue datos.gob.es, which acts only as a distributor.
  • Licence. The three dataset pages declare Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0): sharing and adapting for any purpose, even commercially, are allowed.
  • Obligations. Give appropriate credit, provide a link to the licence and indicate if changes were made. The project credits Ajuntament de Barcelona and states that the data were converted and transformed to RDF.
  • Compatibility. The three datasets share the same licence, so the integrated dataset can be published under CC BY 4.0. Wikidata content is released under CC0 and does not restrict reuse.

6. Optional requirements

  • R5 - Documentation. D3 has high documentation (field-by-field description and change log), with the discrepancy in the species column names. D1 and D2 have medium documentation: metadata in the portal and a list of fields.
  • R6 - Multiple data sources. Medium. The application combines three datasets of the same publisher (shelters, fountains, trees), joined through neighbourhood and district codes, and enriches them with Wikidata. A possible extension is the population by neighbourhood from Open Data BCN.

7. Observations for the data preparation

  • Join key. The three datasets use the same two-digit codes for neighbourhoods and districts (for example 03). The names differ: D3 uses upper case without apostrophes (for example "LA NOVA ESQUERRA DE LEIXAMPLE"), so the join will use the codes.
  • Missing values. 10 fountains have no neighbourhood; in the first records of the tree file, tipus_aigua and catalogacio are empty and data_plantacio is empty in 75% of the records.
  • Species names. D3 includes varieties and cultivars (for example "Washingtonia filifera var. robusta" and "Fraxinus angustifolia 'Raywood'"), which must be normalised to species before linking to taxa. D3 writes "Platanus × acerifolia", while the Wikidata item found is named "Platanus × hispanica", so synonyms have to be mapped.
  • Town name. D1 writes the town as "Barcelona" and "BARCELONA".
  • Shelter category. It has to be derived from the name of the place, and the duplicated records of the same facility have to be handled when counting.
