Dataset Requirements Assessment

Project Name: Personalized Chicago Park & Art Explorer

Datasets:

Chicago Park District Facilities

Chicago Park District Park Art

R1. Data are in the smart cities domain Satisfied

The selected datasets contain information about urban infrastructure, public amenities, and cultural assets within the City of Chicago. This falls under the Smart Cities domain (specifically Urban Planning, Recreation, and Culture).

R2. Data are available as a CSV file Satisfied

Both datasets are published on the Chicago Data Portal and are natively downloadable in CSV format.

R3. Data have an open license so it can be published Satisfied

There is an interesting legal nuance regarding these datasets:

  • The Data Portal: The Chicago Data Portal explicitly labels these datasets as "Public Domain". The Portal's Terms of Use allow for the creation of secondary or derivative applications using the data, provided a specific disclaimer of liability is included in the application.
  • The Source Agency: The dataset's source is the Chicago Park District's website Terms of Use, which contain strict standard copyright restrictions ("No Content... may be copied, publicly displayed, reproduced...").
  • Conclusion: This strict language applies to the Park District's creative website content, not the raw factual data hosted on the City's Open Data Portal. To fully satisfy this requirement and comply with the City of Chicago's Open Data Terms of Use, we will publish the RDF data under an open license and include the mandatory City of Chicago disclaimer in our final application's footer.

R4. Data can be easily linked with generic real-world entities Satisfied

The datasets offer excellent opportunities for Linked Data integration based on their native schema:

  • Entities in our datasets that can be linked to other datasets:
    • Parks (via the PARK and PARK_NO columns):
        Can be linked to their corresponding Wikidata or DBpedia URIs.
    • Artists (via the ARTIST column):
        Can be linked to their respective Wikidata or DBpedia URIs.
    • Locations (via X_COORD, Y_COORD, and the_geom Points):
        Can be linked to GeoNames or OpenStreetMap spatial entities.
  • Entities in other datasets that can link to ours:
    • Public Transit Datasets:
        Can link to our generated RDF Parks as the "closest recreational facility".
    • Tourism/City Guide RDF datasets:
        Can link to our Art Sculptures as "Points of Interest (POI)".

R5. Documentation exists for the data (Optional) Partially Satisfied

While the City of Chicago provides a basic metadata portal, the official documentation is somewhat sparse. For exmaple, there is no detailed data dictionary explaining the specific categorizations used within the FACILITY_N (Facility Name) or FACILITY_T (Facility Type) columns. We will derive these categorizations directly from the CSV values during the RDF transformation process.

R6. Data can come from multiple data sources (Optional) Satisfied

We are using two distinct datasets that will be mapped and interconnected securely using their shared PARK_NO identifier and spatial coordinates. Additionally, enriching the data with external nodes like Wikidata acts as a third external data source.
