Dataset Requirements

Dataset

We work with MyLA311 Service Request Data 2025, an open government dataset maintained by the City of Los Angeles Information Technology Agency. Each row describes a 311 ticket opened by residents or city staff (bulky item collection, graffiti cleanup, street repairs, and similar municipal issues).

Because the live portal dataset is refreshed daily and contains a very large number of records, the repository keeps a CSV extract for 2025. Column names and semantics match those published on the portal.

Portal page: Los Angeles Open Data — MyLA311 2025

R1. Data are in the smart cities domain

MyLA311 records how citizens interact with city services and how departments respond to infrastructure problems. That kind of operational city data clearly belongs to the smart cities domain.

R2. Data are available as a CSV file

The Los Angeles Open Data portal (Socrata) exposes the full table as a downloadable CSV, which is the format used in this project.

R3. Data have an open license so it can be published

Licensing is open: the portal publishes the dataset under CC0 1.0 Universal (Public Domain Dedication). There are no copyright barriers to converting the data to RDF, linking it, or redistributing derived Linked Open Data.

R4. Data can be easily linked with generic real-world entities

Linking is straightforward because tickets carry both map coordinates and named administrative areas used by the City of Los Angeles.

Entities in the dataset that can be linked

  • Los Angeles (city): every request is scoped to the City of Los Angeles.
  • Address / street: fields such as Address, HouseNumber, Direction, StreetName and Suffix identify physical streets.
  • Coordinates: Latitude and Longitude (and Location) pinpoint where the issue was reported.
  • ZipCode: US postal areas inside Los Angeles.
  • Council district: CD (district number) and CDMember (council member name).
  • Neighborhood council: NC / NCName.
  • Police precinct: PolicePrecinct (LAPD area).
  • Responsible agency: Owner (e.g. LASAN) and AssignTo identify the department or work group handling the ticket.

Entities in other datasets that can be linked

Those local identifiers can be aligned with well-known external resources, for example:

  • Wikidata / DBpedia entries for the City of Los Angeles and, where published, council districts or LAPD divisions.
  • GeoNames (or similar gazetteers) for zip codes and place names.
  • OpenStreetMap ways and nodes for streets and reported locations.
  • Official City of Los Angeles open datasets describing neighborhood councils, council districts or departmental organization charts.

R5. Documentation exists for the data (optional)

The dataset is accompanied by official documentation on the Los Angeles Open Data portal. The “About this Dataset” page and the column catalogue describe the main fields (SRNumber, RequestType, Status, RequestSource, CreatedDate, and the geographic columns), together with their types and brief definitions. Status values such as Open, Closed and Pending, as well as the request channels, are also documented. This is sufficient to prepare an RDF mapping without having to infer the meaning of the attributes. There is no standalone technical report beyond the portal metadata; even so, we regard the requirement as satisfied to a high degree.

Documentation: MyLA311 2025 – column descriptions and metadata

R6. Data can come from multiple data sources (optional)

The published file is a single CSV issued by one organisation (the City of Los Angeles), so the requirement is not met in the strict sense of combining several independent datasets. At the same time, the records themselves are not produced by a single intake process. According to the portal, MyLA311 collects reports from 3-1-1 telephone calls, call centres, email, the mobile application, the website and related channels (RequestSource). Fulfilment is also shared among departments, as shown by Owner and AssignTo (for example LASAN versus other bureaus). On this basis we consider R6 satisfied only to a medium degree: multiple sources contribute to the table, but they are already merged by the city before publication.
